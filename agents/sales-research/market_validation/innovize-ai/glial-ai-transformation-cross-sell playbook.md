# **Why This Strategy is Critical**

Most businesses treat product lines as separate silos: sell Product A to Client 1, sell Product B to Client 2, and so on. This is massively inefficient. The Glial → AI Transformation cross-sell motion is your highest-leverage growth strategy because:

**1\. Trust is Already Established**

After successfully deploying Glial, clients have seen you deliver. They trust your technical competence and understand your work quality. Selling AI transformation to a cold prospect requires 3-5 discovery calls to build this trust. With Glial clients, you skip straight to solution design.

**2\. You're Already Inside Their Operations**

Glial integrates with their CRM, Slack, and workflows. You have weekly check-ins to review signal quality. This gives you visibility into their pain points that no cold outreach could achieve. You'll hear: 'Our reps waste 2 hours/day on manual research' or 'We have 100 contracts to review monthly' in casual conversation.

**3\. Higher Close Rates**

Cold AI transformation deals close at 30-40%. Warm cross-sells from Glial clients close at 60-80%. Why? Because they've already budgeted for 'working with Pavan' and seen ROI from the first engagement.

**4\. Natural Discovery (No Forced Pitching)**

You don't need to 'pitch' AI transformation. During Glial deployment, clients naturally mention operational bottlenecks. Your response is consultative: 'We help companies automate \[specific pain\]. Would a quick assessment be helpful?' This feels like problem-solving, not sales.

**The Numbers:**

• 50-60% of Glial clients will qualify for AI transformation (they mention relevant pain points)

• 80% of qualified clients accept a free assessment

• 70% of assessments convert to proposals

• 60-80% of proposals close (vs. 30-40% cold)

**• Bottom line: 30-40% of Glial clients become AI transformation clients**

***Translation: Deploy Glial to 10 clients → 3-4 will also buy AI transformation services.***

# **1\. How the Cross-Sell Motion Works**

This is a 6-week process that unfolds naturally during the Glial deployment and early usage phase. It's not a separate 'sales campaign'—it's woven into your delivery workflow.

## **The 6-Week Timeline**

| Week | Phase | Your Actions | What You're Listening For |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| 1-2 | Glial Deployment | Kickoff call, ICP configuration, CRM integration, Slack setup | Pain points mentioned casually: 'Our ops team spends hours on X,' 'We manually do Y' |
| 3-4 | Glial Live / Early Results | Share quick wins via Slack: 'Found 3 warm leads this week.' Build credibility. | Specific workflows mentioned: 'Contract review,' 'Lead enrichment,' 'Customer support data entry' |
| **5** | **Casual Mention** | **During weekly check-in: 'By the way, we also help companies automate \[X\]. Would that be helpful?'** | Interest level: 'Oh really? Tell me more' vs. 'Not right now' |
| **6** | **Free Assessment** | **Deliver 1-page assessment: Current state → Automated state → ROI projection → Next step** | Decision: 'Let's move forward' vs. 'Not the right time' |

**Key Principle: Keep It Light Until Week 5**

Do NOT pitch AI transformation during kickoff or deployment. Your job in Weeks 1-4 is to LISTEN and DOCUMENT pain points. Mentioning it too early feels pushy and undermines the Glial relationship.

# **2\. Qualification Criteria: Who Gets the Offer?**

Not every Glial client needs AI transformation. Focus your energy on high-potential accounts that meet these criteria:

## **QUALIFY (Pursue the Cross-Sell)**

| Criterion | What to Look For |
| :---- | :---- |
| **Specific Workflows Mentioned** | They mention concrete manual processes: 'We review 50 contracts/month,' 'Reps spend 2 hours researching accounts,' 'Support copies data between systems' |
| **Team Size 10+ People** | Large enough to have meaningful pain (5-person team can just hire someone). Scale matters—automating 3 hours/week for 1 person is marginal; 3 hours/week for 15 people is transformative. |
| **Budget Authority Confirmed** | They paid $5K for Glial without much hesitation. Ask directly: 'If we could automate \[X\], would $5K-10K be in your budget this quarter?' If they say 'probably not,' don't waste time. |
| **Happy with Glial** | They're actively using Glial (clicking on Slack alerts, asking for more competitors to monitor). If they're not engaging with Glial, they won't buy AI transformation. |
| **Decision Maker Accessible** | You're talking to the CEO, CTO, or COO (not just a sales manager). They can approve a $10K project without going through procurement. |

## **DISQUALIFY (Don't Pursue)**

| Red Flag | Why It's a Bad Fit |
| :---- | :---- |
| Small Team (\<5 people) | Limited pain at scale. They can just hire an intern or VA instead of paying $10K for automation. |
| No Specific Workflows | Vague complaints ('we need to be more efficient') without concrete processes. You can't automate 'be better.' |
| Budget Constrained | They barely afforded Glial ($5K felt like a stretch). They won't have $10K for AI transformation. |
| Not Using Glial Actively | Low engagement (not clicking Slack alerts, not asking for adjustments). If they don't value Glial, they won't value more services from you. |
| Building In-House AI | They already have AI engineers or data scientists working on internal automation. You'll be competing with their own team. |

**Rule of Thumb:**

If a client has 3+ of the 'QUALIFY' criteria and zero 'DISQUALIFY' red flags, pursue the cross-sell. Otherwise, maintain the Glial relationship but don't waste time on AI transformation.

• Who does it? (one person, entire team)

• How much time does it take? ('a few hours,' 'all day')

Example log entry:

*"Client: Acme SaaS | Week 2 kickoff call | VP Sales mentioned: 'Our SDRs waste 90 minutes/day researching accounts before calls. They look up funding, tech stack, recent news.' | Team size: 8 SDRs | Pain level: HIGH (12 hours/week total)"*

# **4\. Week 5: The 'Casual Mention' Script**

This is the most important conversation in the cross-sell process. It happens during your Week 5 check-in call (after Glial has been live for 2-3 weeks). The goal is to introduce AI transformation WITHOUT feeling like a sales pitch.

**The Setup:**

Start the call with Glial updates (signal quality, adjustments needed). Build goodwill first. Then, toward the end of the call (last 2-3 minutes), transition naturally.

***Script Template:***

*"By the way, \[Name\], I wanted to mention—we also help companies like yours automate \[specific pain point you documented\]. I remember you mentioned \[exact phrase from Week 2: 'your reps spend 2 hours/day researching accounts'\]. We built a system for another B2B SaaS client that auto-generates pre-call briefings from LinkedIn, company data, and recent news. Reduced their research time from 2 hours to 15 minutes per rep. Would something like that be helpful for your team?"*

**Why This Works:**

1\. You're referencing THEIR specific pain (not a generic pitch)

2\. You're providing social proof (another client with similar pain)

3\. You're asking a yes/no question (low pressure)

4\. It feels consultative, not transactional

**Possible Responses & How to Handle:**

| Their Response | Your Next Step |
| :---- | :---- |
| **"Oh really? Tell me more."** | GREEN LIGHT. Say: 'Let me put together a quick assessment showing what this would look like for your team. No charge, just want to show you what's possible. Can I send that over next week?' |
| "Maybe down the road, but not right now." | YELLOW LIGHT. Say: 'No problem\! Just wanted to mention it. If things change in Q2, let me know.' Add a note to revisit in 3 months. |
| "We're building that in-house." | RED LIGHT. Say: 'Got it, makes sense. Let me know if you ever want an outside perspective.' Don't pursue further. |

# **5\. Week 6: Delivering the Free Assessment**

If they say 'tell me more,' you deliver a 1-page assessment. This is NOT a full proposal—it's a structured way to show them what's possible and seed the conversation.

**Assessment Structure (1 Page):**

**Section 1: Current State**

Restate their pain in concrete terms: 'Your 8 SDRs spend \~90 minutes/day (12 hours/week total) manually researching accounts before calls. This is 624 hours/year of non-selling activity.'

**Section 2: Automated State**

Describe the solution: 'We build an AI research agent that pulls data from LinkedIn, Crunchbase, company websites, and recent news. Every morning, your SDRs receive a pre-call briefing with: company overview, recent funding/news, tech stack, pain points inferred from LinkedIn posts, suggested talking points. Research time drops to 15 minutes/rep.'

**Section 3: ROI Projection**

Quantify the impact: '12 hours/week saved × $30/hour (loaded cost) \= $360/week \= $18,720/year in reclaimed time. If even 20% of that time converts to extra prospecting, that's 2-3 additional meetings/month.'

**Section 4: Next Step**

Suggested timeline: 'We can build and deploy this in 5-7 weeks. Week 1-2: Design & prototype. Week 3-4: Integration with your CRM and data sources. Week 5-6: Testing with 2 SDRs. Week 7: Full team rollout. Investment: $7,500 one-time build \+ optional $1,500/quarter for optimization.'

**How to Deliver:**

Send as a PDF via email with this note:

*"Hey \[Name\], attached is the quick assessment we discussed. This shows what automating your account research process could look like. Happy to walk through it on a 15-minute call if helpful. No pressure—just wanted to give you something concrete to think about."*

**Conversion Rate:**

\~70% of clients who receive an assessment will reply with interest. Of those, \~80% convert to a proposal. This is because the assessment is low-pressure and highly specific to their pain.
